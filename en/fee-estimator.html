When should you request a legal opinion on a Turkish law matter?

If you are a foreign company considering a transaction with a Turkish counterparty, planning to enforce a contract in Turkey, or dealing with a regulatory issue affecting your Turkish operations, a written legal opinion from a qualified Turkish lawyer provides documented guidance you can share with your board, investors, or compliance team. It is particularly valuable before signing contracts, commencing arbitration, or making investment decisions that touch Turkish jurisdiction.

International arbitration in Turkey — what foreign parties need to know

Turkey is a signatory to the New York Convention and has a modern arbitration framework aligned with the UNCITRAL Model Law. Turkish courts have developed a consistent body of case law on arbitrability, enforcement, and challenges to awards. For foreign parties with disputes involving Turkish counterparties, Turkish-seated arbitration under ISTAC rules or a neutral seat (ICC, HKIAC) with Turkish-qualified co-counsel is often the most effective path to resolution and eventual enforcement.

Enforcing a foreign arbitral award in Turkey under the New York Convention

Turkey ratified the New York Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards in 1992. Turkish courts are generally receptive to enforcement applications, though grounds for refusal under Article V — including lack of proper notice, non-arbitrability, and public policy — are assessed carefully. Proceedings typically take between 6 and 18 months depending on the civil court's caseload and whether the respondent opposes the application. Early engagement with Turkish counsel after an award is issued significantly improves asset tracing and enforcement outcomes.

Why arbitration clause drafting matters for Turkey-connected transactions

Many cross-border disputes involving Turkish parties arise not from bad faith but from poorly drafted contracts that fail to address governing law, seat of arbitration, language of proceedings, and the scope of arbitrable disputes. A defective clause can render an arbitration agreement unenforceable in Turkey or create jurisdictional challenges that delay resolution by years. Investing in specialist drafting at the outset is significantly less expensive than litigating clause validity mid-dispute.

Turkish residence and work permits for foreign professionals and investors

Turkey offers several residence permit categories suited to foreign investors, retirees, remote workers, and employees of Turkish companies. Work permit applications are processed by the Ministry of Labour and Social Security and typically require coordination between employer and employee. For those considering Turkish citizenship by investment, the programme requires a qualifying real estate purchase or fund investment, combined with a formal application through the Directorate General of Migration Management. Processing times and documentation requirements change periodically; working with a qualified Turkish lawyer ensures your application is complete and correctly filed at the outset.

International divorce and custody matters involving Turkish courts

Cross-border family disputes involving Turkey are often complicated by questions of which country's courts have jurisdiction, which country's law governs the marriage, and whether a foreign judgment or custody order will be recognised in Turkey. Turkey is a party to the Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction, which affects how cross-border custody disputes are handled when one parent relocates a child to or from Turkey without consent. Early specialist advice is important in these matters, as procedural missteps can significantly affect outcomes.
