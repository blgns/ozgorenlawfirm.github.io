International Arbitration in Turkey — Legal Framework & Enforcement

A technical overview of international commercial and investment arbitration within the Turkish legal system — addressing proceedings before ICC, ICSID, UNCITRAL, and HKIAC.

Legal Analysis

Navigating the Arbitration Landscape in Türkiye

Turkey's arbitration framework is primarily governed by the International Arbitration Law (MILPA) and is closely aligned with the UNCITRAL Model Law. This alignment ensures that cross-border disputes are handled with a level of predictability and procedural rigor required by international commercial standards.

The efficacy of arbitration in Turkey relies heavily on the 1958 New York Convention. Under this treaty, Turkish courts are mandated to recognize and enforce foreign arbitral awards, provided they meet the requirements of Article V. However, the process of Exequatur (Recognition and Enforcement) is not automatic; it requires a technical application to the competent Turkish court to convert the award into an enforceable judgment.

The "Public Policy" (Kamu Düzeni) Exception: One of the most critical nuances in Turkish enforcement law is the "Public Policy" exception. Turkish courts may refuse enforcement if the award is deemed contrary to the fundamental legal principles of the Republic of Türkiye. This includes not only statutory law but also the "universal principles of law" and "moral values."

For foreign investors, Investment Treaty Arbitration serves as a critical mechanism for the protection of assets. Turkey's extensive network of Bilateral Investment Treaties (BITs) allows investors to seek recourse before tribunals such as ICSID or UNCITRAL when facing issues like unfair treatment, indirect expropriation, or breach of Fair and Equitable Treatment (FET) standards.

Understanding the intersection of Turkish procedural law and international rules is essential. This includes the careful drafting of arbitration clauses to avoid "pathological" agreements — clauses that are too vague or contradictory — which often lead to protracted jurisdiction battles in local courts.

"The bridge between a successful arbitral award and a tangible recovery is the precise application of the New York Convention and a rigorous analysis of Turkish public policy."

Technical Blueprint

The Process of Recognition & Enforcement in Türkiye

1. Documentation & Formalities

Before filing for exequatur, the following technical requirements must be met:

  • Original award or a certified copy.
  • Apostille certification (per the Hague Convention).
  • Notarized Turkish translations of all documents.

2. Judicial Review (Limited)

Turkish courts cannot review the merits of the dispute. The court only examines:

  • Validity of the arbitration agreement.
  • Compliance with New York Convention Article V.

3. Execution Phase

Once finalized, the award acquires the status of a Turkish court judgment, allowing for:

  • Asset seizure and foreclosure.
  • Enforcement via the Execution Offices (İcra Daireleri).

Technical FAQs

Frequently Asked
about Arbitration
in Türkiye

Objective answers to common questions regarding the Turkish arbitration framework.

What are the primary advantages of arbitration in the Turkish context? +
Arbitration provides neutrality, confidentiality, and the ability to appoint arbitrators with specific technical expertise. Most importantly, awards are globally enforceable in 170+ countries via the New York Convention.
How does the New York Convention operate in Turkey? +
Since 1992, Turkey has applied the 1958 New York Convention. This means Turkish courts generally recognize foreign awards as binding, provided they do not violate "Public Policy" (Kamu Düzeni) or lack proper notice.
Which institutions are most relevant for disputes involving Turkey? +
The ICC (International Chamber of Commerce) is the primary forum for commercial disputes. For investment claims against the State, ICSID is the standard. Additionally, ISTAC is increasingly used for disputes with a local nexus.
What is the typical duration of these proceedings? +
Timelines vary. A standard commercial arbitration may take 12–24 months. Investment treaty cases are more complex and can last 3–5 years.
Can legal representation be coordinated across multiple jurisdictions? +
Yes. International arbitration often requires a "Co-Counsel" model where a Turkish firm handles local law and enforcement, while international firms manage the substantive strategy. Our practice is structured to serve as the Turkish arm of cross-border legal teams.
