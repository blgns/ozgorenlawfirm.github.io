02 — Legal Framework

The Technical Basis of Practice

Our practice is grounded in the international standards of dispute resolution, specifically the alignment of Turkish law with global arbitration norms.

International arbitration in Turkey is governed by the International Arbitration Law (MILPA), which follows the UNCITRAL Model Law. This ensures a predictable environment for foreign parties, where the courts' role is primarily supportive rather than intrusive.

A central pillar of our practice is the 1958 New York Convention. As Turkey is a contracting state, foreign awards are recognized and enforced via Exequatur proceedings, provided they do not violate the mandatory rules of the jurisdiction.

The "Public Policy" (Kamu Düzeni) Nuance

In the context of Turkish law, the Public Policy exception is the most critical ground for refusing the enforcement of an award. We provide technical analysis to ensure that the award's formulation and the proceedings' conduct minimize the risk of such objections during the exequatur phase.

Technical FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the New York Convention applicable in Turkey?+

Yes. Turkey ratified the 1958 New York Convention in 1992. This means Turkish courts are obligated to recognize and enforce foreign arbitral awards, subject to the limited grounds for refusal listed in Article V of the Convention.

What is the role of the Turkish courts in international arbitration?+

Under the MILPA and the UNCITRAL Model Law, the role of national courts is "supportive." This includes assisting in the appointment of arbitrators, granting interim measures, and finally, the recognition and enforcement (exequatur) of the award.

Which arbitral institutions are most common for Turkish disputes?+

The ICC is frequently used for commercial disputes. For disputes involving a local nexus, ISTAC (Istanbul Arbitration Centre) is increasingly common. UNCITRAL rules are often used in ad hoc proceedings.
